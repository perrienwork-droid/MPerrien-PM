Why I made it: I kept hunting for my own references

My role: the whole thing, end to end. I chose the problem, defined the structure, curated and distilled the content, designed the system, and shipped it myself.

Good frameworks were saved across scattered bookmarks, notes from courses faded after a month, useful things sat somewhere I could not quickly find again. Not always lost, but it usually took far too long to get back to the thing I needed.

I also wanted one place that covered the disciplines together, UX, product, marketing, and content, instead of a different site for each. On real work they overlap constantly, so it helps to see them side by side. And I wanted every topic in one format I already know, so finding what I need is quick.

What it is: a working library, not a course

Noir Book grew into a fairly big library. 127 topics across the four disciplines, each with the idea, a method with a template, and a real example. You arrive with a problem, search, find the framework, and use it in a couple of minutes. No signup, no lessons to unlock.

How I built it: mostly editing and structuring

I started from the frameworks I already used in product and UX, listed them out, then settled on one page structure for every topic: the core idea, a practical method with a template, and a real example. Writing each page meant pulling from the strongest sources and distilling them into that same tight format, cutting the padding so what is left is usable mid-project. Most of the work was that editing and structuring, turning scattered, long-winded material into one consistent, findable shape.

For UX especially, there are courses that go deeper on the theory, and that is fine. But on a live project you often need the practical version more than the deep one, and that gap is where this library comes from.

Later I added topics people asked for, some outside my own day-to-day experience. Since that knowledge is well established and public, the job there was the same as everywhere else: read the good sources, fit them to the structure, make them usable. The person who requested each one told me whether it actually helped.

How it went: fun to make, and it stuck

Honestly, it was a lot of fun to build, and it turned out genuinely useful. The best sign was that people I shared it with kept coming back with "oh nice, can you add this too?" That kept it growing, and it is how a good share of the topics got added.
